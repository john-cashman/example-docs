# Water

Water is the simplest and most essential drink. This guide covers choosing the right water, improving its taste, and staying properly hydrated throughout the day.

{% hint style="info" %}
Most adults need around **2 litres (8 cups)** of water per day, though this varies with activity level, climate, and body size.
{% endhint %}

## What you need

- A clean glass or reusable bottle
- Water (tap, filtered, or bottled)
- Optional: ice, lemon, cucumber, or mint for flavour

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Choose your water

**Tap water** is safe and convenient in most regions. **Filtered water** removes chlorine and sediment for a cleaner taste. **Sparkling water** adds carbonation for those who find still water less appealing.

Check your local water quality report if you are unsure about tap water in your area.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Chill or serve at room temperature

Cold water is more refreshing during exercise or in hot weather. Room-temperature water is gentler on digestion and often preferred as a daily staple.

Add ice if you want it cold quickly, or keep a jug in the fridge.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Add flavour (optional)

Plain water can be made more interesting without adding sugar. Squeeze in a wedge of lemon, add a few cucumber slices, or drop in some fresh mint leaves.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Avoid adding large amounts of fruit juice or sweeteners — these turn water into a sugary drink with a very different nutritional profile.
{% endhint %}
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Drink consistently throughout the day

Sip water regularly rather than drinking large amounts at once. Keeping a bottle on your desk, in your bag, or near your bed makes it easier to build the habit.

Thirst is a late signal — if you feel thirsty, you are already mildly dehydrated.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## Variations

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Still" %}
Plain, uncarbonated water. The most versatile option — pairs with any meal and suits all-day hydration.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Sparkling" %}
Carbonated water with no added sugar or flavourings. A good alternative to soft drinks. The bubbles can help some people drink more water overall.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Infused" %}
Still or sparkling water with added fruit, herbs, or vegetables. Popular combinations include lemon and ginger, cucumber and mint, or strawberry and basil. Let the ingredients steep for at least 30 minutes in the fridge before drinking.
{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

<details>
<summary>Signs of dehydration</summary>

Common signs include dark urine, headaches, fatigue, and difficulty concentrating. Mild dehydration can affect mood and cognitive performance before you notice physical symptoms.

Pale yellow urine is a simple indicator of good hydration. Dark yellow or amber suggests you need to drink more.
</details>
