# Coffee

A well-brewed cup of coffee starts with fresh beans, the right grind, and water just off the boil. This guide walks through a simple pour-over method you can repeat every morning.

{% hint style="info" %}
For the best flavour, use whole beans and grind them just before brewing. Pre-ground coffee loses aroma quickly once the bag is open.
{% endhint %}

## What you need

- Fresh coffee beans (medium roast works well for pour-over)
- Burr grinder or pre-ground coffee (medium grind)
- Kettle
- Pour-over dripper and paper filter (e.g. V60 or Chemex)
- Mug or server
- Scale (optional but helpful)

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
#### Heat the water

Fill the kettle and heat water to about **93–96°C** (200–205°F). If you do not have a thermometer, bring the kettle to a boil, then let it sit for 30 seconds before pouring.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Boiling water straight onto the grounds can taste bitter. Slightly cooler water extracts more evenly.
{% endhint %}
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Rinse the filter

Place the paper filter in the dripper and set it over your mug or server. Pour hot water through the filter to rinse away paper taste and warm the vessel. Discard the rinse water.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Weigh and grind

Use roughly **15 g of coffee per 250 ml of water** as a starting ratio. Grind to a medium consistency — about the texture of coarse sand.

Adjust to taste: finer grind and longer contact time yield a stronger cup; coarser and shorter yield a lighter one.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Bloom the grounds

Add the ground coffee to the filter and level the bed. Pour about twice the weight of the coffee in water (e.g. 30 ml for 15 g) and wait **30–45 seconds**. You should see the grounds swell and release gas — this is the bloom.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Pour in stages

Pour the remaining water slowly in circles, keeping the bed wet but not flooding it. Aim to finish pouring within **2 to 3 minutes** total brew time.

Let the last drips fall through, then remove the dripper.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
#### Serve

Give the coffee a quick stir if brewing into a server, then pour into your cup. Drink while hot, or add milk if you prefer — no rules required.

For a stronger or weaker cup next time, change the coffee amount by a gram or two rather than grinding much finer.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## Other methods

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="French press" %}
Use a **coarser** grind and a ratio of about 1:15 coffee to water. Add grounds and water, stir, place the lid, and steep for **4 minutes**. Press the plunger down slowly and pour immediately so the coffee does not over-extract.
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Drip machine" %}
Follow your machine’s fill lines. Use a medium grind and fresh filter. Brew as soon as the cycle finishes — coffee left on a hot plate can taste stale within minutes.
{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

<details>
<summary>Storing beans and grounds</summary>

Keep whole beans in an airtight container away from light, heat, and moisture. Use within **2–4 weeks** of roasting for peak flavour.

Ground coffee goes stale faster; only grind what you need for each brew.
</details>
